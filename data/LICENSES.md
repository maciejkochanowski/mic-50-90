# Rights for software examples and source data

MIT applies to MIC-50-90 software. It does not relicense third-party data, articles or fonts.

| Material | Distribution and attribution |
|---|---|
| Small factual published-summary and count examples | DOI links, source locations and analyst assumptions accompany the inputs. Source terms remain applicable; no complete source article, figure or table is supplied. |
| Delma et al. example row | The source article carries CC BY-NC 4.0. `examples/published_table/SOURCE.json` and `examples/returned_counts/SOURCE.json` retain this separate licence and the source hash. |
| Other publication-derived examples | Preserve their source DOI, metadata and any source-specific terms. Some source articles use CC BY-NC and others CC BY; the software licence does not override either. |
| Selected federal surveillance aggregates | FDA/USDA NARMS and CDC remain the identified data sources. Preserve attribution and source terms; do not assume that all records submitted through NCBI are government works. |
| Constructed demonstration inputs | Identify them as controlled examples rather than observed biological data. |
| Bundled fonts | Their licence files are distributed with the font resources. |

Full workbooks, raw phenotype repositories, manufacturer guides, publisher articles, editorial materials and historical research reports are outside the software package. ATLAS raw workbooks and derived histogram tables are not redistributed.

The [source map](SOURCES.md) points to the provenance records delivered with runnable examples. Do not remove these records when redistributing an input. A derived value is not automatically free of its source terms. Nothing in the package implies endorsement by the source agencies or authors.
